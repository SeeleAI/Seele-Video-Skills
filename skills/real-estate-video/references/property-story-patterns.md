# Property Story Patterns

Choose one structure and preserve the property's real layout. Do not combine unrelated locations into an apparently continuous walkthrough.

| Pattern | Best use | Spatial arc |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival-to-retreat | Residential listing tour | verified context → exterior → entry → shared spaces → private spaces → close |
| Guided walkthrough | Agent-led tour with consent | orientation → route preview → sequential spaces → verified highlights → CTA |
| Architecture and craft | Designed building or restoration | massing/context → circulation → light → verified materials/details → relationship to site |
| Interior sequence | Interior design showcase | orientation → room flow → verified focal points → material/detail studies → usable experience |
| Development and amenities | Approved development marketing | site/context → verified built or planned spaces → verified amenities → access/circulation → disclosure → CTA |
| Lifestyle-in-place | Permission-cleared occupied scenario | authentic routine → spatial support → verified feature in use → transition → quiet close |

## Hook selection

Use one truthful, filmable hook: an authorized exterior approach, a threshold reveal, an actual sightline, changing natural light captured at the stated time, a verified architectural detail, or an orientation move that clarifies layout. Aerial footage requires legal flight access and current surroundings. Before/after material requires matched, authorized evidence and accurate labeling.

## Shot design

- Establish orientation before close detail.
- Follow actual adjacency through doors, corridors, stairs, or exterior paths.
- Mix stable wides, eye-level movement, and details without using extreme lenses to exaggerate scale.
- Show windows and views only from their real positions; do not replace skies, scenery, or neighboring context deceptively.
- Treat lighting changes, virtual staging, construction visualization, and reconstructed scenes as illustrative and disclose them.
- Include people only when identities, actions, and usage rights are approved.

## Timing

Use five structural beats: orientation, arrival, spatial flow, verified detail, and close. The local builder allocates the exact requested duration across these beats. For complex properties, create multiple approved packages or expand the editorial plan after the canonical five-beat pass rather than cramming unsupported rooms into one cut.
