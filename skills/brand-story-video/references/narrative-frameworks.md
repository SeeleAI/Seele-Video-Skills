# Narrative Frameworks

Choose one architecture. Do not stack several arcs into a short film.

| Architecture | Best use | Arc |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Founder-led or early-stage brand | lived problem → decision → first proof → continuing commitment |
| Customer transformation | Supported customer outcome | friction → attempt → credible shift → next step |
| Craft/process | Makers and operational excellence | standard → difficult choice → careful work → result |
| Mission/anthem | Values-led organization | shared tension → people acting → visible evidence → invitation |
| Milestone | Anniversary or impact review | distance traveled → contributors → current proof → future commitment |
| Day in the life | Product integrated in routine | real context → friction → unobtrusive help → regained agency |

## Hook selection

Use one of these: a sensory detail, hands at work, an unresolved human problem, a concrete contrast, an in-motion moment, or an unexpected connection. The hook must be truthful, intelligible without brand knowledge, and visually achievable.

## Beat allocation

- **0–10%:** Hook and situation.
- **10–45%:** Tension, stakes, or unmet need.
- **45–75%:** Action and evidence of change.
- **75–90%:** Meaning or outcome.
- **90–100%:** Quiet CTA and brand signature.

Use 2–6 seconds per shot. Hold longer only when a performance, line, or transformation needs room. Show values through choices and behavior; do not substitute slogans for evidence.
